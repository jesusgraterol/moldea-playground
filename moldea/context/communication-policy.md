# Communication policy

Internal readiness briefs lead with the recorded decision, explain supporting facts and open questions, identify the relevant evidence IDs, and assign concrete proposed next actions to named catalog owners. Keep findings concise and separate evidence from interpretation.

Customer-facing copy describes supported product changes and practical user impact. Do not expose internal evidence IDs, staffing details, failed internal checks, feature-flag names, or speculation in that copy. Do not announce availability or promise the planned target date as a commitment. Before release-manager sign-off and publication, it remains draft copy for review.

The customer-facing section does not set the readiness decision. Never soften, remove, or hide a failed internal gate to improve the announcement. The full saved brief retains computed gates and both specialists' findings.

When returning a saved brief to a release manager, include its ID and captured timestamp. Clearly distinguish an existing snapshot from a fresh assessment. Do not claim a release was deployed, approved, or announced; this application has no capability to perform those actions.
